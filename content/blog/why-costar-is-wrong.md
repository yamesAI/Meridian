Co-Star is a well-designed app with excellent UX, strong branding, and a user base of millions. It is also not doing astrology in any way that traditional astrologers would recognize.

This isn't a dismissal. It's a technical observation. Understanding the difference helps you understand why a transit reading generated by The Meridian is a different category of thing.

## What Co-Star Actually Does

Co-Star maps your natal chart placements to a library of text. Sun in Scorpio → text block A. Moon in Pisces → text block B. Mercury in Sagittarius → text block C. The daily horoscope is a combination of which placements are being activated by current planetary positions, mapped to pre-written descriptions.

This is useful for one thing: giving you general language for your natal tendencies.

It is not useful for: knowing what's actually happening to you right now, identifying timing windows, understanding which transits are high-priority versus low-priority, or knowing what your Saturn Return means for the specific arc you're in.

## What Traditional Astrology Actually Does

Traditional astrology — the system developed across 2,000 years by Vettius Valens, Guido Bonatti, William Lilly, and their contemporaries — does not start with natal placements and generate text. It starts with a question: *what is the current state of this person's chart in relation to the current sky?*

This requires several things:

**Transit calculation**: Where are the planets today, and what exact angles are they forming to your natal planets? An opposition from transiting Saturn to your natal Venus at 2° orb, applying, is a high-priority event. A square at 8° orb, separating, already happened.

**Profection year**: Which house is activated this year? All transits to the time lord of that house are amplified.

**Essential dignity**: What condition is each transiting planet in? A Jupiter transit from exaltation delivers very differently than a Jupiter transit from detriment.

**Sect and reception**: Is this a day chart or night chart? Do the two planets involved have a positive relationship to each other in the chart? Mutual reception changes the outcome significantly.

None of this can be reduced to template text. It requires calculation specific to your chart, your current date, and the interaction between the two.

## The Profection Year Alone Changes Everything

Here's the single most important thing: two people with identical natal charts but born exactly 12 years apart will have completely different experiences of the same transit — because they're in different profection years.

A Jupiter transit to natal Venus means one thing in a 7th house profection year (relationships, partnerships) and a completely different thing in a 2nd house profection year (money, personal resources). Co-Star cannot distinguish these. It doesn't track profection years at all.

The Meridian calculates your profection year first, identifies your time lord, and then interprets every transit in relation to what's actually activated in your chart this year. The result is a reading that feels specific because it *is* specific — not because it's written in an intimate voice, but because the technique demands specificity.
